The Story Behind Bella Flor's Handmade Jewellery

What 'handmade' actually means when it's stitched into the price tag rather than just the marketing copy.

Every Knot Tied by Hand

There's no machine involved anywhere in a Bella Flor bracelet. Each piece starts as loose lengths of cord, measured and cut by hand, then knotted one loop at a time until the pattern — twisted, braided, or woven — is finished. A single chunky bangle can take considerably longer to knot than a slim bracelet simply because there's more cord to work through, strand by strand.

Why We Don't Mass-Produce

Machine-knotted cord jewellery exists, and it's usually cheaper for exactly that reason — a machine can tie a thousand identical knots an hour. The trade-off is that every piece comes out the same, and the knot tension is set once for every size of wrist. Working by hand means we can adjust tension as we go, catch a flaw in the cord before it's knotted into the final piece, and finish each bracelet slightly differently rather than identically.

The Materials We Choose and Why

We use premium-grade cord specifically because it holds colour and shape better than budget alternatives, and it's paired with metal end caps, toggle clasps and lobster clasps rather than cheaper plastic fittings, which is why a Bella Flor piece doesn't feel or look disposable after a few weeks of wear.

What "Handmade" Means for the Bracelet on Your Wrist

In practical terms: the knot tension is judged by feel, not a machine setting, which is part of why our pieces hold up well to daily wear and why a sliding closure can be adjusted without the whole bracelet coming loose. It also means no two pieces are perfectly identical — small, deliberate variation is part of what handmade actually looks like, not a flaw to be engineered out.
